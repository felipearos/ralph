## The verdict: do not split

#2942 is a server prefactor — the four shared status cores (status, Wantlist, like, rating, used by both books and music) take a member id instead of reading a cookie, and the like becomes a desired-state write. It arrived in the `oversize` bucket. It should not have.

`isOversize` (`frontier/next-tasks.ts:1320-1325`) is two thresholds: more than 8 acceptance checkboxes, or 3+ of four surface regexes over the whole body. #2942 has **7** checkboxes, and it matched three surfaces on prose: `web` (the web server actions), `api` via `/\bserver\b/` ("a server prefactor"), and `mobile` via the single sentence "A later ticket adds mobile routes over the same cores." That last one is the tell — **the sentence that trips the surface count is the one deferring that surface to another ticket.** The frontier's own comment says being offered a ticket that needs no split costs a marker, which is the right trade; this is just the first time on this project it actually fired.

The mapper settled it against the code. All four cores are in **one file**, `src/server/queries/externalStatus-write.ts`, sharing one private `authorize()` (66-75) that does the rate limit, `requireMember()` and the suspension check. The transports are two 67-line adapters (`actions/readStatus.ts`, `actions/listenStatus.ts`), each holding all four concerns. So a per-concern split gives two children the same three files and the same two conformance tests; a per-layer split makes the second child a no-op PR. 9-12 files, one unit. Left `ready-for-agent` in staffing group `book-write-cores`; every open non-spec sibling of #2934 carries a group already.

Since the ticket is labelled, the human-edits rule forbids replacing its `Acceptance criteria`, and there was no need to: the criteria are testable as written. The whole product of this unit is **one amending comment** (`"amends": true`) carrying the map and eight constraints the criteria imply but do not name.

## What the spec reviewer caught, and one of it mattered a lot

Three findings, all upheld after I checked them in the code myself:

1. **P1 — my point about the review-visibility conformance test was wrong, and the fix I prescribed was a bug.** I had written that a web like action reading `review` to keep its flip adds a new unguarded scan, and that the lexical rule means the published-review predicate must sit in the scanning declaration. In fact `quickLikeExternalCore` and `quickRateExternalCore` **already hold an allowance of 1** (`review-visibility-conformance.test.ts:330-331`), and `MOVED_SCANS:494-501` records those scans as having moved *out of* `quickLikeBook`/`quickLikeMusic`. More to the point, they sit under the "author's own review" category, whose doc at 305-310 says these reads **must not** filter `is_hidden`: "a finder that cannot see a moderator-hidden review lets its author silently write a second one over the top of it". My advice would have hidden a moderator-hidden title review from its own author and made the like write insert a duplicate row. The comment now says the test stays green, names `getUserExternalStatus` (allowed at 329) as the read for the flip, and says in so many words not to guard an own-row read.
2. **P2 — I had turned "the like takes a wanted value" into a required Zod schema change.** It is not: a typed `liked` argument satisfies criterion 3, as `setReviewLikeCore(userId, reviewId, liked)` does. Requiring it on `quickLikeBookSchema`/`quickLikeMusicSchema` breaks `packages/shared/src/schemas.test.ts:73,81` and creates an ordering trap — the web transport must parse before it authenticates, but cannot know `liked` until after it authenticates and reads the row. The comment now gives both options with their costs and says the schema-driven body belongs to the mobile ticket.
3. **P2 — a constraint I had missed entirely.** `externalStatus-write.ts:61-64` documents that every core calls `authorize()` *inside* its own `try`, because the pre-#398 modules ran the limiter outside theirs and a throw escaped as a rejected promise instead of a failure the UI could show. Moving the limiter to the transports without a `try/catch` around it therefore breaks criterion 2's "behave exactly as before". That is now its own numbered point.

It also corrected two line refs (the conflict `set` is 135-138 not 130-135; 142 is `revalidateUserStats`, not a second `revalidatePath`) and rightly killed my overstated claim that a mocked-db test "stays green with the id ignored" — a `vi.fn()` chain *can* assert call arguments; what it cannot show is the stored row and the replay, which is what criterion 6 asks for.

**Worth carrying forward:** the reviewer's brief was the comment text itself, pasted verbatim with "the worktree contains none of this" stated up front, and it worked — it read the real files and checked me. On a comment-only unit the review is not owed by §3's letter (no split, no rewritten criteria, no new promotion), and running it anyway is what caught the P1.

## The decision: no split

Loggd's rule did not fire — 7 acceptance criteria (under 8) and one surface:
"mobile API" is the v1 API, with no mobile client code in scope (#3072 and
#3073 own the app side) and no schema change at all.

The mapper nonetheless bounded it at 15–22 files, which is the top of what the
coders have been shipping here, so I did consider the one seam there is:
create+edit as one child, delete+offer-read as another. Two reasons it is the
wrong seam, and the first is the one that generalises:

1. **Both children would land in `book-log-api`.** The group already serialises
   them through CI and the merge queue, so the split buys no parallelism — only
   a second CI and merge cycle and a duplicated gate/transport/snapshot
   scaffold.
2. The delete half reuses the Books gate, the transport order, the
   row-ownership checks and the not-found shape the create half establishes.

**A third reason worth recording for whoever meets this next: a split cannot
express its own dependency edge.** `add-blocker` takes a plan-local `ref` only
in `blockedBy`; its `issue` must be a real number (`triage/plan.ts:69`,
`:327-333`). So in a two-child split neither child can be declared blocked by
the other in the same plan, and the frontier could hand the delete half to a
coder before the core existed. A split whose children are ordered needs its
children to already exist, or it needs a seam whose children are independent.

## The thing I got wrong, and what caught it

Criterion 6 says a signed-out caller is "refused as on the other book writes",
and the book writes disagree: `status`, `wantlist`, `rating` and `like` are
`withAuth` (401), while `books/[id]/progress/route.ts:42-47` is
`withOptionalAuth` answering `bookNotFound()`, with a doc comment arguing a 401
"says the route exists".

I drafted a **Decision** section settling it as the bare not-found, cited to
`CONTEXT.md`'s Book Viewer State line "a signed-out reader … get the bare
not-found every book route answers", and recorded it in the plan's `decisions`.
The round-2 spec review refused it, correctly: that sentence describes a batch
*read*, contrasted with Viewer Media State answering empty, and read as a
universal rule about routes it is **false at HEAD on four of the five book
writes**. Nothing in `CONTEXT.md` or `docs/adr/**` rules between them — Thin
Transport, Book Library Write and Reading Progress are all silent. I was
picking the minority code precedent and attributing it to a document.

What I posted instead decides nothing: it names both patterns with their line
numbers, says no document rules between them, and reads the criterion at its
word — four routes on `withAuth` against one, so `withAuth` and a 401 — then
says plainly that preferring the `progress` pattern is a **change to criterion
6 and a question for Felipe on the issue, not a coder's choice**. The ticket
stays ready, which is right: this is one line plus a test, not a blocker.

**Lesson for the next triager.** A glossary sentence whose subject is a read is
not a rule about writes, and a "documented rule" the shipped code contradicts
on most of its instances is not a rule — it is a conformance gap. Both
readings felt citable until the reviewer counted the routes. When a cited rule
and the majority of the code disagree, the plain reading of the ticket's own
sentence beats both, and the residue is a question, not a decision.

Round 1 was worth its tokens too: it caught that I had told the coder the
after-the-delete recheck "already exists" (it does not, for a book) and that an
empty hide set on `deleteReviewCore` would let a book route delete any of the
member's logs by id. Both would have sent the coder the wrong way.

## READ THIS FIRST: `false` is a claim, and an absent field is not

The one real bug in this branch, and **both review axes found it independently** — which is the strongest signal I have seen for keeping the two separate.

`viewerFieldsOf` copied `state.watched` onto a poster's row after a write settled. At **season** scope that value is the released-episode progress the *sheet* paid a TMDB fetch for, and the batched poster read cannot see released counts. So: rate a fully-watched season → check appears → invalidate → refetch answers `false` → check vanishes. A visible flip on the happy path.

The shallow fix is a scope guard. The right fix was to notice **why** the two surfaces could disagree at all: `watched: boolean` gave "not watched" and "not answered" the same spelling, so the surface that *had* an answer had no way to know the other would contradict it. `watched` is now `optional` and **absent** at season scope. That turns a comment into a type, and the client guard ("a season writes no watched") reads as a consequence rather than a special case.

**Generalisable:** when two surfaces compute the same field from different evidence, the one that cannot compute it must be able to say so in the type. A defaulted `false` is how a cache flip gets built.

## The decision worth arguing with

**A season's `watched` is not answered.** A season is watched when every *released* episode is, and only `getSeasonDetails` knows the released count; one TMDB fetch per season would undo the batching the endpoint exists for.

The **spec axis pushed back hard and was half right**: `getBatchSeasonWatchedCounts` already does the watched half in one grouped query, and `getSeasonDetails` is Next-cached, so "N-call read" only bites on a batch of many *distinct* seasons. I kept the decision — the open question is a bound on provider reads per request, which is a design choice, not a line of code — but filed **#1150** with that analysis in it rather than restating my own reasoning. A reviewer who improves your argument against you should end up in the follow-up.

## The review earned its cost twice over

1. **It killed my note 1.** I had read AC1's "pushes as a show List reply does" as
   requiring a per-List push tag, because removing `url` collapses
   `buildTag("comment-reply", [undefined])` to the bare `comment-reply`. True, but the
   tag is read **only by the browser** (`public/sw.js:92`); the native transport sends
   no tag at all (`push-transport/native.ts:24-31`). So the collapse cannot affect the
   app, which is the only place a book notification belongs — and my own note 4 had
   already put browser push out of scope. The amendment was a scope increase
   (`push-payload.ts` plus a test plus a new AC7 pin) dressed as a clarification. It is
   now one sentence in the out-of-scope section saying `push-payload.ts` needs no edit.
2. **It caught a test instruction that would not work.** The add integration test
   replaces `notifyAfterResponse` with a bare `vi.fn()` (`:29`, `:33-35`), so the
   delivery is not in the mock's call arguments at all — the coder has to invoke the
   captured callback (the second argument at `listComment-write.ts:200`) and assert what
   it returns. "Assert the captured delivery" would have sent them looking for something
   that does not exist.

It also supplied the homes for AC2 (`notify.test.ts`, book fixture at `:462-467`) and
AC3/AC4 (`notification-read.integration.test.ts`, `bookRow` at `:123-126`, Books-preview
cases from `:188`), and upgraded note 6 from optional to near-required: a fixture with
`media_type: "book"` does not typecheck against `mobile/hooks/useNotifications.ts:38`
without a cast, so a properly typed AC5 test widens that union.

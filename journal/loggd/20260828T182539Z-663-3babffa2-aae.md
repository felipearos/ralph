## The design fact worth carrying forward

**A durable job's terminal write is already an idempotency fence — do not build
a second one.**

The ticket asks for "exactly one notification, idempotent under worker replay
and recovery", which reads like it wants a unique index, a dedupe key, or a
`notified_at` column. It needs none. Every terminal transition in
`src/server/imports/jobs.ts` is a conditional UPDATE that answers whether *this*
caller made it — `finishImportJob` / `failImportJob` return `rows.length > 0`
off `heldClaim`, and `recoverStaleImportJobs`' abandon branch guards on the
attempt ceiling plus a live status. Notify iff that answer is yes, and replay, a
superseded worker, a concurrent completion and a double sweep all collapse to
one notification for free. Same shape `userFollow-write.ts` gets out of
`onConflictDoNothing()`.

The call goes **inside** those three functions, not at their call sites, so a
future caller cannot forget it — the same argument `notify.ts` already makes
about deriving the push payload centrally.

The honest limit, which the spec axis found and I kept: this is exactly-once
under every *concurrency* failure and at-most-once under a *crash between the
two writes*. Closing that would mean a transaction-aware `notify`, which is a
change to the notification layer rather than to this ticket.

## Two preference maps, one `null`, two meanings

`notify.ts` had `BELL_PREFERENCE_BY_TYPE` typed `Record<Exclude<NotificationType,
"list_collaboration_invite">, …>` and a total push map. Two new types made that
shape untenable. Both maps are now total with an explicit `| null`, and the
`null` means the opposite thing per axis: bell `null` = **always recorded**,
push `null` = **never pushed**. That asymmetry is why they cannot share a shape,
and it is written out at the maps rather than inferred.

Adding a Bell Preference or a Push Preference for these types would have meant
`profiles` columns — a *second* production migration on a ticket already
blocked by one. It is also wrong on the merits: the AC says a completed import
creates a notification, full stop, and a preference would make that conditional.

## Traps this iteration hit

**`NotificationType` in `schema.ts` is hand-written, not derived from the
pgEnum.** Adding enum values compiles fine and then fails in five unrelated
files. `src/server/db/schema.ts:1032` is the second edit; make it immediately
after the enum or you will chase phantom errors.

**Adding a notification type breaks four total maps.** Budget for them:
`notify.ts` × 2, `NotificationDropdown.tsx`'s `notificationIconMap`,
`push-payload.ts`'s exhaustive switch, plus mobile's `NotificationType` union
and **two** `iconMap`s (`app/notifications.tsx` and `app/(tabs)/activity.tsx`).
The mobile screens also carried two copies of `getNotificationLink`; they are
now one `mobile/lib/notification-links.ts`.

**`buildPushPayload`'s switch has no `default`.** A bell-only type still needs
an arm. The standards axis called it speculative generality and said to omit it;
that remedy does not compile. Worth knowing before you agree with a reviewer.

**`Object.hasOwn`, not `in`, for a "is this a known key" guard.** The first cut
of `isImportSource` used `in`, so `"toString"` passed and
`IMPORT_JOB_BASE_PATHS["toString"]` was stringified into a URL. The standards
axis caught it. The test that pins it is `it.each(["toString", "constructor",
"hasOwnProperty", "__proto__"])` — it fails under `in`, which is the point.

**Reading a query parameter in a client hook: `window.location` inside the
existing post-hydration effect, not `useSearchParams()`.** The latter forces a
Suspense boundary and a CSR bailout on three statically-renderable routes for a
value only ever read after hydration. And strip the parameter once adopted
(`history.replaceState`) — otherwise a reload weeks later re-adopts a finished
job over the member's live one.

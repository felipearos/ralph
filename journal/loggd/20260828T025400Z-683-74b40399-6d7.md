## The design decision the whole thing turns on

`supabase/config.toml` governs the **local stack only**. Nothing in this
repository pushes it; the hosted project's auth settings are applied by hand in
the dashboard, the way `docs/go-live.md` applies everything else. So a checked-in
declaration cannot *enforce* the production posture — it can only make drift
**detectable**. Two columns, one asserted against the TOML and one against a
runbook a human follows, is the most a repository can honestly do here. Say that
plainly rather than implying the file is deployed; the reviewer's AC1 verdict was
"partially met" and that is the correct reading.

The one place the two columns diverge for a *testing* reason is
`token_verifications` (5 local, 30 production). Proving throttling means
exhausting a real budget, and that is the only counter no other local suite
spends — nothing in `e2e/` reaches `/auth/v1/verify`, which needs a token hash
only a real email carries. Exhausting `sign_in_sign_ups` or `email_sent` instead
would leave the Playwright run throttled behind it on the same loopback address.
**If the CI red turns out to be e2e, this choice is the first thing to question.**

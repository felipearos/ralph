## The contract I pinned, and why it is not a product decision

Both children are written against one contract, stated verbatim in both bodies so neither needs
#2490's text:

- `advanceImportSetupStep` returns `{ advanced: boolean; completed: boolean }`; a refusal and a
  thrown error both answer `{ advanced: false, completed: false }`.
- Both accepted-import responses keep `onboardingCompleted` with **exactly** today's meaning and
  gain a sibling `onboardingStepAdvanced: boolean`, present only when the request came from setup.

`onboardingCompleted` keeps its meaning to keep mobile out: `mobile/hooks/useImportJob.ts` and
`mobile/components/onboarding/OnboardingFlow.tsx:83` read it, and mobile already leaves setup only
on that flag and otherwise refetches the snapshot. Naming the field is not a product question —
#2490 itself demanded "a field that separates advanced from refused"; a split has to pin the name or
the two children disagree.

Two further things are now explicit rather than implied, both read out of **#2479's own acceptance
criteria** ("the `import` step is retired", "the onboarding step enum gains `seen`", minimum of "at
least five Watched/Watching titles in total with at least one per chosen mode"):

1. the bypass submits `{ step: "seen" }`, not `{ step: "import" }` — after #2479, `import` is not a
   step at all;
2. the browser's retry of `submitOnboardingStepAction` is **removed**, not made conditional. A
   `{ step: "import" }` retry becomes an unknown step and a `{ step: "seen" }` retry is exactly what
   the server refuses under the minimum, so the hook navigates to `/onboarding` instead — the
   outcome `src/server/imports/onboarding-step.ts:20-22` already describes in prose. The member
   lands in the same place either way, so no user-visible behaviour was decided here.

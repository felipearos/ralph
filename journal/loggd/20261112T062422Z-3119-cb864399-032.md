## What the two review rounds were worth (3 blocking in round 1, 2 in round 2, all real)

Round 1:

1. **AC2's mutation check does not bite under naive seeding.** `favorite_show.tmdb_id` holds
   a djb2 hash of the book id (`favorite-write.ts:28-38`), so two *different* books hash to
   two different values, and "two favourite books held by two members" passes even for a
   query matching on `tmdb_id`. It only fails if both rows share one `tmdb_id` with different
   `external_id`s — legal, since the unique key is `(user_id, tmdb_id, media_type)`. Without
   that sentence in the comment, AC2 is a test that proves nothing.
2. **My own draft contradicted itself on `displayName`**: it chose a shape with a display name
   and then told the coder to reuse `getExternalLikes`, which maps through `toShowMember` and
   cannot carry one.
3. **I had invented "signed-out members reads are allowed by the body".** AC6 says a
   signed-out viewer gets the bare not-found from *both* reads. Corrected to the `!user ||`
   guard on both routes.

Round 2, and these are the two findings I would not have reached alone:

4. **Dropping `rewatched` from the book member type is the decision, not keeping it.** I had
   read the body's field list as governing and therefore dropped `rewatched` as meaningless
   for a book. But `mobile/components/MediaPeopleScreen.tsx:280,304` renders it off a
   `ShowMember`-typed row (`mobile/hooks/useShowSubpages.ts:52-60`), so a *narrower* book type
   is what forces a mobile change. The superset — every `ShowMember` field, plus `displayName`,
   with `rewatched: false`, which `toShowMember` already hard-codes (`external.ts:30`) —
   satisfies both halves of the body's self-contradictory sentence and decides nothing.
   **This is the generalisable move**: when a body names a shape and also enumerates fields
   that do not match it, the superset keeps the product question out of the ticket.
5. **"Either fix the shared helper or write a book-only read" was wrong**, even though AC7's
   literal words allow it. `read-friends-activity.pglite.test.ts` pins the book scope's answer
   as `"watched"`/`"watching"` (`:726-731`, `:813-822`) *and* pins the read at one database
   statement (`expect(statements).toBe(1)`, #1648, `:755-757`). Book words turn the first red;
   calling `viewerExclusion` inside the counted read turns the second red, since it runs its
   own lookup (`block.ts:165-170`). The comment now requires a book-only v1 read with the
   exclusion resolved at the route and passed in, as `membersForScope` does
   (`media-subresources.ts:324`).

Round 2 also caught me overstating `viewer-exclusion-conformance.test.ts`: it matches the
retired *names* in source text (`:22-33`), so it would not catch an inline `userBlock`
subquery — which is exactly what `followedBy` is, and it passes on `main`. The guidance
(take the exclusion) was right; the claim about what the test enforces was not.

## The authority question I pushed back on, and why

Round 1 said the `displayName` reading is a product decision no documented rule settles and
that #3119 therefore belongs to a person. I disagreed and kept it `ready-for-agent`: the
superset in finding 4 means no decision is being made at all. Round 2 agreed explicitly once
the superset replaced the narrowing. Worth remembering as a pattern — a reviewer calling
something a product decision is sometimes a signal that the *draft* decided something, not
that the ticket requires it.

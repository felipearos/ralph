## What I decided and why

**The observed condition is gone, and nothing in this repository can carry a
fix for it.** Two independent reasons, both measured rather than assumed:

1. *The premise is stale.* The learning records 2026-10-08, when the open list
   was six issues and `frontier/next.ts --take-one` returned `ticket: null`. At
   `origin/main` 41f92cc8f today there are **60 open issues, 33 of them
   `ready-for-agent`**, seven of those claimed by coder loops (`ralph-wip`,
   `rafa-wip`, `remo-wip`, `rulo-wip`, `testo-wip`). Every ticket the learning
   names as the reason the frontier was empty is closed: #1996, #2203, #1956,
   #1911 completed, #1940 not planned. Exactly one open issue carries
   `needs-triage` — #2818 itself. A coder would have nothing to reproduce.
2. *There is no code here to change.* The learning's own `Workaround` line says
   "none for a loop — `ALL_BLOCKED` is the only correct ending". A drained
   frontier is a backlog-supply condition; the loop already ends correctly, and
   every lever (what gets filed, whether a `ready-for-human` ticket is
   relabelled, whether an empty frontier should notify instead of ending quietly)
   lives in `felipearos/ralph`, which no Loggd loop can open a PR against.

So the remaining action is an operator's and it is a choice no documented rule
settles: retire the learning outright
(`publish-journal.ts --close loggd-backlog-human-gated --scope project`), or keep
it and file the harness-side question on `felipearos/ralph`. That is the
`ready-for-human` reason stated on the issue.

**Why not a close.** A close needs `done-in` (a merged PR meeting every
criterion) or `duplicate-of`. Neither exists: the condition resolved through
ordinary backlog growth, not through a PR, and no other issue covers it. The
honest verdict is the one that puts it in front of a person.

**Body left alone.** Its acceptance criteria are the clerk's placeholders
("specify a reproducible verification criterion before marking this ticket
ready-for-agent"), so nothing goes unbuilt by leaving them, and `ready-for-human`
is a statement about *where* the work lives, not about how it is specified.

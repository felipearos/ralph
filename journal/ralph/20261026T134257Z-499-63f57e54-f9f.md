## What I decided and why

#499 ("clerk: add a code-context comment to every filed ticket") came out of the
intake with no state label and none of the five managed sections — a prose
Behaviour list of five bullets and nothing a coder could finish. Checked against
`origin/main` through the mapper: nothing of it exists (`codeContext`,
`code-context`, ffmpeg all absent from the repo).

It is **oversize** by this project's rule: it changes the clerk gate and store,
the supervisor's prompt and model routing (`ralph.sh`, `roles.ts`, `models.*`),
a permission rail, the project config and the docs — well past three areas, and
its Behaviour list unpacks to far more than eight criteria. So: split into five,
along the areas, in dependency order.

- #502 config (`clerk.codeContext`, warnings not problems, `clerkRules`, preflight, README, fixture)
- #503 the store queue and its state machine (key+app, report id, four counters, `handedAt`, prune in the gate)
- #504 `clerk/code-context.ts`: the result contract as constants, `prepareCase`, `cleanupCase`, the handover
- #505 the `code.context` stage, its prompt, its rail file and the `ralph.sh` branch
- #506 result validation, the single post, the bounded failure paths

**The order is not native.** `add-blocker` can only name an existing issue and
all five were created in one plan, so there are no edges between them. What
orders them is their ascending numbers plus the single staffing group
`clerk-code-context` — all five in one group on purpose, so they are worked one
at a time. Each body opens with "Child N of 5" and says what must have merged
first. A follow-up could add the native edges; I did not split the plan to get
them, since splitting a plan to dodge a tool's shape is exactly what §4 forbids.

## The review did the work

Four rounds, and the first three all refused the split. What they caught, and
what the children say now:

1. Round one: the malformed-config path as first written would have gone through
   `clerkProblems`, which `parseProjectConfig` throws on — the clerk would have
   stopped, the opposite of the ticket's "absent or malformed, nothing changes".
   Also: nothing marked which case was in flight; `attempts` had two owners; the
   result contract's values lived nowhere.
2. Round two: `clerkProblems`' unknown-key list would have refused the block
   anyway; `caseDir(key)` gave two cases of one `both` ticket one directory; the
   rails as stated had no owner; the handover sat where settled reports could
   still be waiting.
3. Round three: the stage name `code-context` would have exported as
   `RALPH_MODEL_CODE-CONTEXT` — not a shell identifier, and `ralph.sh` evals it;
   the top-level `default` block is judged as a coder's lineup, so a stage only
   the clerk runs fails `--validate`; a Jira post that throws could stop the
   loop; `hasComment` can never match a body with no `en Slack:` marker.
4. Round four passed it as written. Its three remaining notes (the project-level
   `default` block, a write-port method of its own for the shadow record, the
   emoji character set) were folded in without a fifth round.

Every one of those would have been a coder's dead end rather than a review
finding. Worth the four rounds.

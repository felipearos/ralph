## What I decided and why

#415 ("pr-medic: adopt a loop's PR when its owning loop has died") arrived
unlabelled with 4 ACs. The mapper confirmed none of it is on `main`: `assess()`
skips every loop branch by prefix (`pr-medic/gate.ts:245`), `shepherdable()`
keeps a PR only for the loop its provenance line names
(`frontier-gate.ts:203`), and nothing anywhere reads another loop's liveness
when choosing PRs. But the work crosses three of this project's areas —
durable state (`loop-state.ts`), the frontier's owner-side probes
(`frontier-gate.ts` + `prompt.md` §1) and the pr-medic role — so it is
oversize. Three children, a strict chain:

- **#423 loop-state: which loops are unattended.** One pure predicate over
  `snapshot()`'s reports plus `loop-state.ts unattended --json`, no behaviour
  change. `ready-for-agent`.
- **#424 frontier-gate: a loop does not take back an adopted PR.** The shepherd
  probe, the resumable-claim probe and the local-recovery step yield to another
  loop's `-wip`, plus the `prompt.md` §1 steps and the multiple-claim halt.
  `ready-for-agent`.
- **#425 pr-medic: adopt an unattended loop's red or conflicting PR.** No state
  label: it is blocked by both siblings and a plan cannot name an issue number
  it is creating. Its `## Blocked by` says so, and the next triage pass records
  the native edges and promotes it.

All three share the staffing group `pr-adoption`: they are a chain, so no
parallelism is lost, and they overlap on `README.md` and the prompts.

## The decisions, with evidence

- **#424 lands before #425.** The owner's claim is its `-wip` on the TICKET,
  which outlives the iteration and the 120-second lease
  (`claim-lease.ts:27`), and `decide()`'s resumable-claim probe
  (`frontier-gate.ts:330-336`) spawns an iteration that checks out
  `<loop>/<n>-*` and pushes without consulting `shepherdable()` or any lease.
  Adopting before that yields would leave a window holding exactly #415's race.
- **"Stopped on purpose" is the stop kind AND the `control-stop` event.**
  `ORDERLY_STOP_KINDS` (`controls.ts:235-237`) excludes only `interrupted`, so
  the API and remote paths may stop a loop as `failed`.
- **The predicate answers about a loop NAME, with a live worktree winning.**
  `snapshot()` reports per worktree, so an old worktree left behind would mark
  a live loop unattended and get its branch force-pushed.
- **The claim lease is not the fence.** After 30 minutes the owner's lease is
  `expired` (anyone may take it) or absent. It is still taken — #415 asks for
  it, and it keeps a coder off the ticket — under the medic's supervisor pid,
  the only pid `renewLease` accepts.
- **Adoption must be endable.** The resumed-case check has to precede the
  `agentOwner` skip, or the medic never sees its own adopted PR again once it
  goes green. Handback: label, lease, one comment, no push.
- Out of scope: a coder adopting through a frontier bucket (`FrontierBucket` is
  `"ready" | "triage"`; #415 names pr-medic as preferred). No `decisions` block
  in the plan: #415's own text settles the adopter and the 30-minute threshold,
  so nothing here was a product question I decided.

## Spec review — three rounds, all of which changed the split

1. The claim lease was the wrong fence (a 30-minute-old lease is `expired`, not
   `dead`); nothing ended an adoption once started; the push fence has a second
   refusal (the `prMedic.branches` glob) and needs `labels` in `PR_FIELDS`;
   `failed` can be an orderly stop.
2. The merge order was backwards, and a yielding owner would trip
   `prompt.md:271-273`'s multiple-claim halt and stop the loop for a human.
3. The predicate keyed per worktree could mark a live loop unattended, and the
   iteration cannot see a PR's labels at all until `labels` joins §0's
   `gh pr list` (`prompt.md:192`).

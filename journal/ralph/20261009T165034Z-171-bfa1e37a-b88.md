## Why I did not open a competing PR

#289 covers all nine acceptance criteria, and on both points where our
implementations diverge it is the better one:

1. It takes `flock(2)` through **`bun:ffi`**. I had read the ticket's
   `flock -n` wording and AC 9's "spawns nothing but the redactor" as being in
   tension, and deviated to an exclusive-create pidfile with a pid liveness
   check, declaring it. `bun:ffi` dissolves the tension instead of trading one
   requirement away, and that is simply the right answer. Worth remembering as
   a pattern: when two requirements look mutually exclusive, the FFI route to
   a libc call is sometimes the thing that satisfies both.
2. It re-checks the lease **inside every snapshot upsert**, so a lease that
   lapses between beats cannot be followed by a write. Mine checked the lease
   on the heartbeat only, which leaves a 15-second window.

I also diverged on the driver — `Bun.SQL` with `max: 1`, because this repo has
neither `postgres` nor `pg` and the test harness is PGlite — where #289 used
postgres.js. Either is defensible; theirs matches the ticket's prose.

I compared the two for anything worth salvaging and found **nothing**. The only
thing my branch has that `main` lacks is `bridge-contract.test.ts`, which pins
`ralph.bridge`'s three CHECK lists against a TypeScript copy in
`contracts.ts` — and `main` never declares that copy, so there is nothing to
drift. No follow-up ticket is owed, and I filed none.

Two defects I found in my own work and fixed, both of which would apply to any
implementation of this ticket and are worth knowing:

- **The heartbeat needs a busy flag of its own.** Sharing one with the read
  pulse means a slow local read can starve the lease renewal and cost the
  bridge a lease it is holding perfectly well.
- **The heartbeat must read `ralph.viewer` before it writes its report.** The
  other way round, a viewer whose row arrives between beats stays invisible for
  a further 15 s — most of its own 120 s window.
